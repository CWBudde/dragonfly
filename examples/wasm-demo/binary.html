V-shaped and S-shaped

The two families behave differently and the curve shows why. A V-shaped function — v1 to v4, with v3 the paper's default — is symmetric about zero: it flips a bit on the magnitude of the step regardless of sign, so a large step means "change, whichever way you currently are". An S-shaped function — s1 to s4, the logistic family — is monotone: a large positive step drives the bit toward one and a large negative step toward zero.

That difference has a visible consequence in the matrix. A V-shaped run keeps churning bits while steps stay large, and settles as the step clamp shrinks. An S-shaped run commits earlier and is harder to talk out of a bit it has decided on.

The three objectives here are the demo's own; the library's benchmark suite is defined over real vectors. They are deliberately small enough to check by eye — on TargetPattern the answer is literally the stripe every column converges to. The problem data is generated from the bit count alone and never from the run seed, so changing the seed compares two runs on the same problem rather than two different problems.
