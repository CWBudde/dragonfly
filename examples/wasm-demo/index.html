Two things that look odd and are not

The neighbourhood is drawn as a square. It is not a stylisation. The reference implementation's neighbour test is per-dimension — every component of the distance must be within r — so the neighbourhood really is an axis-aligned box, not a ball. A Euclidean shortcut changes the swarm dynamics and degrades convergence without failing any end-to-end test, which is why the demo asks the library for the test rather than reimplementing it.

Dragonflies teleport across the stage. That is the paper's boundary rule: one that leaves the box reappears at the opposite bound and has that step component reset to a fresh random draw. Wrapping is part of DA's exploration, not a bug. Switch Boundary to clamp or reflect to see the alternatives.

The branch mix is mostly one colour, and that is the finding. The neighbourhood radius grows as r = (ub−lb)/4 + (ub−lb)·(t/T)·2, so it passes the width of the whole search box about a fifth of the way into the run. From then on every dragonfly has the food source in range and takes the five-factor step; the local-swarming and Lévy branches belong to the opening iterations. Drag the scrubber back to the start to see them, or raise the iteration count to compress them further.

Above two dimensions the heatmap is a slice: the unplotted axes are pinned at the known minimiser, so the picture passes through the global minimum where one is known and says so when it does not. The swarm's positions are real in every dimension; only the projection is flat.
