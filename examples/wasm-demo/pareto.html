A multi-objective run has no single best answer, so it has nothing to converge to — it has a front to spread out along. MODA keeps that front in a bounded archive and partitions objective space into hypercubes: the food source is drawn from the least populated occupied cell, the enemy and every overflow eviction from the most populated one. That is the entire mechanism that stops the archive collapsing onto one point, and it is what the shading below shows.

How to read this

The axes are the two objectives, not the decision variables. A point is one archive member; down and to the left is better in both. A healthy run spreads points evenly along the front rather than piling them into one corner, which is exactly what the sparse-cell food draw is for.

ZDT3 is the one to watch. Its true front is broken into five disconnected pieces, so it is where a method that quietly favours one region of objective space shows it.

The grid is drawn from the archive's own extent, read through ParetoArchive.GridBounds(), and each point's cell comes from the GridIndex the archive assigned. Nothing here re-derives which cell a point falls in. The archive's roulette exponents (β, γ, δ) are recalled from the MOPSO lineage the paper borrows from rather than read off the author's MODA.m, and the library documents them as unverified; treat the defaults as defaults.
